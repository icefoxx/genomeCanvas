ProfileSeg evaluation report

Name: "Seg" currently means region-level screening of differential profiles, not segmentation of curves into changed intervals.

Status as of 2026-10-26, end of day (code: ProfileSeg v2.4, uncommitted working tree after commit a6fa276). All numbers come from the scripts in benchmarks/ and are detailed in docs/PROFILESEG_COMPARISON.md.

1. Summary

ProfileSeg compares coverage profiles around predefined anchors between two conditions with biological replicates. It reports amplitude (how much signal) and shape (where and how spread the signal is) separately, for the typical region and for each region, and treats replicates — not regions or reads — as the unit of inference.

Main result. The per-region shape tests (shape_kernel, omnibus; shift, directional with an effect size in bp) are calibrated with as few as two replicates per condition on simulated, semi-real and real data, and they detect known changes in two real assays. On the same real BAMs, the closest existing tool (MMDiff2) made false calls in real nulls where ProfileSeg made none, and detected fewer genes in a known positive control.

Main limitations. Small per-site effects are information-limited (50 bp shifts at 2 vs 2); processing batches that coincide with the contrast are detected as differences; the shift effect size is attenuated by up to ~27 % at 2 vs 2 on broad profiles; the aggregate (typical-region) analysis is reliable only under stated conditions. Evidence for some application areas (TF redistribution, cut sites, histone spreading) is simulation-only.

aspect assessment
statistical design replicate-level inference; shape metrics use background-corrected, mass-normalised profiles, which separates shape from amplitude exactly for noise-free multiplicative changes and approximately with noise and imperfect background estimates (checked by amplitude-only scenarios, not guaranteed); per-region p-values come from a null pooled over regions and label assignments, so they depend on the null being comparable across regions (§4, §7)
calibration evidence strong for per-region tests: frozen validations (final round passes every criterion), real nulls in 3 assays (yeast MNase 2v2, PRO-seq 3v3, single-line CUT&RUN 3v3), insertion on real replicates
power per region (20 % of regions changed): 100 bp shifts 0.52–0.97; 50 bp shifts 0.16–0.47 at 3 vs 3 and ≤ 0.16 at 2 vs 2; partial broadening 0.01–0.65 depending on shape and design; coherent ~20 bp shifts detected on real PRO-seq at 2 vs 2
robustness single-replicate artefacts absorbed and flagged; batch structure aligned with the contrast is detected (correctly, but it must be designed out)
practicality bigWig / computeMatrix input; seconds for 5,000 regions; ~100× faster and ~20× less memory than MMDiff2
maturity beta; embedded in GenomeCanvas; single developer; not yet packaged separately

2. Method in brief

component what it does
background correction per sample and region, mean of the outer flanks
amplitude (per region) log2_signal, log2_signal_relative (relative to the bulk of regions, half-sample-mode offsets), log2_enrichment; limma-identical moderated t, BH
shape_kernel Gaussian-kernel (MMD-type) distance between condition-mean mass-normalised profiles, divided by within-condition spread + s0; null pooled over regions × non-observed label assignments
shift projection of each sample's profile on the derivative of the pooled template (locally most powerful for small translations), same pooled null; effect size in bp from a weighted Gauss–Newton template fit
label-invariant s0 s0 from the total spread, identical under every assignment (a per-assignment s0 made the test anti-conservative; found and fixed during development)
diagnostics background ratio, amplitude re-weighting, replicate-wide offsets relative to the group, leave-one-replicate-out retention (≥ 3 per group)
options condition-blind anchor alignment (FunChIP-style shift-only k-mean alignment), descriptive shape classes

3.4 Runtime and memory

Default pipeline (current code, single thread): 2.8–7.9 s for 5,000 regions (2v2–4v4), 11–36 s for 20,000. Peak memory 0.8–1.5 GB (5,000 regions) and 2.7–3.6 GB (20,000). An exact kernel feature map made the default pipeline 19× faster at 20,000 regions (4 vs 4). MMDiff2 on simulated BAMs: 418 s / 7.8 GB (300 regions), 912 s / 6.6 GB (1,000 regions).

4. Overall assessment

Strengths

  1. Addresses a real gap: replicate-level, per-region shape inference that is separated from amplitude, on coverage tracks, at 2–3 replicates.
  2. Calibration is supported by pre-specified criteria on fresh seeds, real nulls in two assays and insertion on real replicates — more than most published tools in this area.
  3. Explains why existing approaches fail (Fisher combination of dependent p-values in MMDiff2; pseudo-replication in region-as-unit tests; amplitude–position mixing in pausing indices) and shows it on real data.
  4. Interpretable outputs (bp, log2), diagnostics, low resource use.

Weaknesses and threats to validity

  1. Simulation-based tuning shares a generative family with the simulation validations (mitigated, not eliminated, by semi-real and real-data checks).
  2. Development history includes an amended selection rule and two fixed defects (per-assignment s0; dependent semi-real noise) before the final validations.
  3. Batch structure aligned with the contrast produces calls; only balanced designs (or batch-aware assignments, in progress) avoid this.
  4. Information limits for small per-site effects at 2 replicates.
  5. Effect-size attenuation of shift at 2 vs 2 on broad profiles.
  6. Aggregate analysis: earlier validation failures, low power at 2 vs 2, unstable on periodic profiles; positioned as descriptive with explicit conditions of use.
  7. Real-BAM comparison so far in one assay and at subsampled depth; MMDiff2 had to run in single-end mode (its paired-end mode fails in 1.30.0).

Readiness. Suitable for internal use and for reporting per-region shape changes with the caveats above. For publication in a mid-tier methods journal: about 85 % ready for a paper centred on the per-region shape tests; the open items are listed in §7.

Design guidance: 1 replicate — descriptive only; 2 — per-region shifts ≥ ~20 bp when coherent, ≥ 100 bp in general; 3 — most effects of interest; ≥ 4 — small per-site effects. Balance processing batches across conditions. Use spike-ins for absolute amplitude claims.

Not suited to: anchor-free discovery; very broad domains; ratio-of-assays questions (Ribo-seq TE); absolute global changes without spike-ins.

7. Gaps: closed and open

Closed in this round: batch-aware label assignments (confounded designs refused; optional within-batch permutation); shift attenuation (V4 passes everywhere); a single-cell-line 3 vs 3 real null (CUT&RUN, 20 splits); kernel bias under amplitude changes (found by validation, fixed); a miscalibrated exploratory test (log2_scale) removed; 19× faster pipeline.

Open: 1. Aggregate width metrics under amplitude heterogeneity (A2/A3) and per-region absolute amplitude in some designs. 2. A second real-BAM comparison with adequate depth (the CUT&RUN data were too sparse). 3. Batch information for public data (inferred here, not recorded in GEO). 4. Stand-alone packaging.
